Local–Global VBD

An experimental opt-in global structural correction for local VBD. The report compares it with legacy local VBD, specialized scalar PCR, sparse-direct VBD, and Kamino PADMM where an exact matching fixture exists.

Reading the report. Smaller named errors are better; motion, contact, settling, and cost must be read together. Work differs by method: Local–Global VBD uses one early 6D correction, PCR corrects eligible translation paths every iteration, and sparse direct couples the articulation every iteration. Focused step times use the median of three no-render trajectories where available; sparse-direct timing falls back to its rendered trajectory. Coefficients are method-tuned only where explicitly stated.

Summary

  • Against legacy local VBD, one global correction reduces cable-closure RMS by 76% in the Slinky and 53% in the dense pile.
  • In the pulley sweep, coefficient-matched PCR markedly improves legacy local VBD. With method-tuned compliant coefficients, Local–Global has the lowest pull-force RMS at 2:1, 4:1, and 20:1; PCR is better at 8:1 and has lower lift RMS through 8:1.
  • The K=1e9 Y-junction reaches effectively zero closure and symmetry error with Local–Global VBD; scalar PCR has no eligible path on this branched topology.
  • The closed cross-slide is the important counterexample: its local constraints are already well converged, so the global pass adds cost without improving table tracking.
  • Specialized scalar PCR is cheaper on eligible open paths. Sparse direct remains the strongest coefficient-matched closure reference on the reproduced linkage fixtures; the method-tuned four-bar G=1 row reaches 0.171 µm RMS versus 0.265 µm for sparse direct. Local–Global VBD targets broader topology and contact coverage with one opt-in correction.

Slinky settle, release, and landing

Long helical chain including the delayed-collapse transient and ground contact. Closure, penetration, and final speed must be read together because a stiffer-looking transient need not be more settled at the final frame.
