Accuracy and remaining limits

Mixed cost changes; no general convergence improvement claimed. High-stiffness droop retains the 0.411 mm maximum final shape error. Ordinary Toy4 passes both pull orders; the stiff variant still fails its slider or extension checks.

Neither route is penetration-free or universally regression-free. In the displayed current pile pair, late RMS speed is 1.758 mm/s (G0) versus 0.952 mm/s (G1), and late cable overlap is 0.3702 versus 0.1434 mm. Slinky and pulley retain more late motion with G1. Lower residual or quieter motion alone does not establish improved physical accuracy.

What still needs fixing

Not regression-free or universally fastest. The droop accuracy regression persists. Ordinary Toy3/Toy4 pass the authored motion screens in both directions, but the stiff Toy4 variant still fails: the current AB control exceeds the 0.5 mm slider limit screen, and stiff BA still fails its authored motion screen. These results are not hidden by the rendered successes.

At equal I5 budgets, G1 costs roughly 1.67–2.31× the local route in the five cable fixtures. It is valuable where its additional convergence allows fewer local iterations; it is not automatically a win everywhere. Higher late motion in slinky/pulley remains an accuracy question, not evidence of a correct or incorrect energy balance by itself.
