Friction that slows spinning and rolling, and holds when it should

Friction is what makes a sliding box stop, a spinning top wind down and a rolling ball come to rest. Newton's VBD solver used to model only the first of these. This change adds resistance to spinning and to rolling, and makes the sliding friction hold firmly when several objects press on each other.

Spinning resistanceA ball or a coin spinning on a table slows down and stops. Before, it would spin forever.
Rolling resistanceA rolling ball loses speed and stops. Before, it rolled on forever.
Steadier gripObjects that should stay put, like boxes stacked on a ramp, now do, instead of slowly creeping.

Spinning resistance matters for anything twisted against a surface: a screw, a bottle cap, an object held between two fingers.
